\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Participants, recordings, and neural preprocessing}

Ten adults (7M/3F; age 22--54) with medically refractory epilepsy participated
during clinical intracranial monitoring at Baylor St.\ Luke's Hospital EMU
\citep{Xiao2024A,Xiao2024B}.
Single-unit activity was recorded from sEEG Behnke--Fried microwires on a
512-channel Blackrock Neuroport system (30~kHz), spike-sorted with Wave\_clus
\citep{Chaure2018}, and manually curated (quality 4--5 retained; $N=10$
hippocampus, $N=9$ ACC).
Patients held unprompted conversations (14.7--70.1~min; mean 20.2~min);
audio was transcribed with WhisperX \citep{Bain2023}, diarised with Pyannote
\citep{Bredin2021}, and manually corrected in Praat for precise word onsets.
For each word $w_t$, spikes were summed over a 500~ms window aligned to
acoustic onset ($-200$~ms for speaker words; 0~ms for listener words),
$z$-scored, and PCA-reduced (90\% variance), giving neural trajectory
$\mathbf{Y}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{T\times k}$.

\subsection*{LLM embeddings}

Word-level hidden-state embeddings were extracted from nine pretrained
autoregressive and masked LLMs, spanning three architecture families: GPT-2
and GPT-2-medium \citep{Radford2019}, OPT-350m \citep{Zhang2022}, BERT-base
\citep{Devlin2019} and RoBERTa-base \citep{Liu2019}, LLaMA-2-7B
\citep{Touvron2023}, LLaMA-3.1-8B \citep{Grattafiori2024} (primary model),
Gemma-2-9B \citep{GemmaTeam2024}, and Mistral-7B \citep{Jiang2023}. All models
were loaded from their public Hugging Face Hub checkpoints via the
\texttt{transformers} library \citep{Wolf2020} (LLaMA-2/3.1, Gemma-2, and
Mistral-7B in bfloat16 precision on a single GPU; smaller models in float32).

For each transcript, every word was encoded with a leading whitespace and the
model's native byte-pair or WordPiece tokenizer, and per-word token spans were
recorded to allow later pooling. Because transcripts routinely exceeded a
model's maximum context length (1024 tokens for GPT-2, up to 8192 for
LLaMA-3.1/Gemma-2/Mistral), the token stream was processed in overlapping
windows (stride = 50\% of the model's usable context) with a single forward
pass per window (\texttt{output\_hidden\_states=True}); for encoder models
(BERT, RoBERTa) \texttt{[CLS]}/\texttt{[SEP]} tokens were added per window and
their hidden states discarded afterward. Hidden states from overlapping
windows were averaged token-wise, and subword-token hidden states belonging to
the same word were mean-pooled to yield one embedding per word at every
transformer layer, giving semantic trajectory
$\mathbf{E}^{(\ell)}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$ at layer $\ell$. Embeddings
were cached per patient/model to disk (as \texttt{[layer, word, dim]} arrays)
so all downstream analyses reused a single extraction pass.
Words within two positions of a sentence boundary or speaker-turn change were
excluded.

\subsection*{Geometric measures}

Let $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$ be either trajectory, with
displacement vectors $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t = \mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_t$.

\paragraph{Curvature.}
The turning angle at interior word $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_t = \arccos\!\left(
    \frac{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t-1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}_t}
         {\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t-1}\|\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_t\|}
  \right), \quad t=2,\dots,T-1.
  \label{eq:curv}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Displacement.}
$d_t = \|\boldsymbol{\delta}_t\|_2$, symmetrised as
$\tilde{d}_t = \tfrac{1}{2}(d_{t-1}+d_t)$ to align with curvature support.

\subsection*{Layer selection}

For each patient $i$, the analysis layer was selected by leave-one-out
cross-validation maximising alignment with neural curvature across held-out
patients:
\begin{equation}
  \ell^*_i = \underset{\ell}{\arg\max}\;
    \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}
    \left|r\!\left(\kappa^{(\ell)}_j,\,\kappa^{\mathrm{neu}}_j\right)\right|.
  \label{eq:loo}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Regression analyses}

All models used OLS with $z$-scored predictors.
To test whether semantic geometry predicts neural geometry, we regressed
neural curvature (or neural displacement) onto the corresponding LLM measure
while controlling for sentence position, turn boundaries, speaker role, word
length, and log lexical frequency:
\begin{equation}
  \kappa^{\mathrm{neu}}_t \;=\;
    \beta_0 + \beta_1\kappa^{(\ell^*)}_t + \beta_2\tilde{d}^{(\ell^*)}_t
    + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\!\top}\mathbf{c}_t + \varepsilon_t,
  \label{eq:reg_neural}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{c}_t$ is the vector of nuisance covariates.

To quantify the unique contribution of word surprisal
$h_t = -\log_2 p(w_t\mid w_{<t})$ (extracted from LLaMA-3.1-8B),
we used sequential $\Delta R^2$: surprisal was added last to a model already
containing $\kappa^{(\ell^*)}_t$, $\tilde{d}^{(\ell^*)}_t$, and
$\mathbf{c}_t$, and the increment in explained variance was tested against a
temporal-shuffle null (50 permutations).

Event-triggered curvature was computed in $\pm5$-word windows around
high-surprisal ($>\!$75th percentile) and low-surprisal ($<\!$25th percentile)
words.

\subsection*{Cross-space decoding}

To assess whether the geometric relationship is bidirectional, we trained
linear ridge-regression decoders to predict neural curvature from LLM curvature
(LLM$\to$Neural) and vice versa (Neural$\to$LLM), each using chunk-level
$z$-scored curvature vectors at the selected layer.
Decoders were evaluated by leave-one-patient-out cross-validation; mean
Pearson $r$ across patients was compared to a shuffle null.
The cross-lag condition used LLM curvature at $t$ to predict neural curvature
at $t+1$ (and vice versa), providing a temporal-asymmetry check.
